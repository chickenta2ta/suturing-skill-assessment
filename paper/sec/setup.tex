\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Data and annotations}
\label{ssec:data}

We use the Suturing task of the public JIGSAWS dataset~\cite{Gao2014}, recorded
with the da Vinci Surgical System on a bench-top model. Eight surgeons (B--I),
with self-reported skill from novice to expert, performed 39 trials in total,
each consisting of four stitches. JIGSAWS also provides stereo video and robot
kinematics, but to keep the method applicable to ordinary surgical videos, we
use only the monocular video from one endoscope camera (capture1).

To rule out leakage, the surgeons are split four and four, each set having one
expert, one intermediate, and two novices. Everything that involves a choice,
namely designing the method, selecting the few-shot examples, and tuning
parameters, is done on the development set (B, E, F, I; 16 trials, 64
stitches). The test set (C, D, G, H) consists of all 19 trials of its surgeons
(76 stitches) and is evaluated once, with everything fixed.

A surgeon licensed in Japan annotated the events and the EASE~\cite{Haque2022}
scores of every stitch, viewing only the same monocular video as the method.

\subsection{Implementation}
\label{ssec:implementation}

The events, keypoints, and needle masks are all obtained from one
general-purpose VLM, GPT-6 Astra~\cite{OpenAI2026Astra}
with medium reasoning effort (\texttt{gpt-6-astra}), used through the OpenAI API
without any weight update. Each request gives the definitions, a few
examples, and the frames to annotate, and the answer is returned as a
structured output. The examples are taken from two other trials of the
development surgeons, which are used for nothing else, including development.
For events, the examples are the frames around each type of event in one of
these trials. The video is processed at 5 fps, 20 s of frames per request,
together with 10 s of frames on each side as context, in which no events are
detected, and the events detected so far in the video as temporal
information. For keypoints and masks, the examples are 20 frames of the other
trial annotated with keypoints and needle masks, and the video is processed at
10 fps, 20 frames per request, with the mask returned as polygons.

\subsection{Baselines and metrics}
\label{ssec:metrics}

As a baseline, the same VLM scores the three hold sub-skills directly. It is
given the frames of the annotated assessment window at 5 fps with 10 s of
context on each side, the EASE criteria with notes defining each quantity, and
three development stitches with their scores as examples, and returns a score,
the probability of each score, and the value of each quantity. The baseline is
thus given favorable conditions: human-annotated windows and scored examples.

We evaluate in two settings. With the annotated assessment windows, scoring is
evaluated apart from event detection and compared with the baseline on the
same stitches. In the end-to-end setting, the events are also detected, and all
four sub-skills are scored automatically. To compare with the labels, each
annotated stitch is paired one to one with the detected stitch whose entry is
nearest to its annotated entry, within the annotated stitch. Event detection
is evaluated by F1 with a tolerance of 1 s.

For each sub-skill, we report the AUC of separating the best score (EASE 3;
for Needle Hold Ratio, 2--3) from the others, as in~\cite{Deo2026}, and the
balanced accuracy of the score, averaged over the scores present in the
labels. For the AUC, our method ranks the stitches by the signed margin of the
measured value to the EASE criterion of the best score, e.g.,
$\min(\alpha_i-90^\circ,135^\circ-\alpha_i)$ for the angle, and the baseline
by its probability of the best score. A stitch that cannot be scored counts as
an error in both metrics. The 95\% confidence intervals are obtained by
bootstrap resampling of videos, since the stitches of a video are correlated;
differences between methods are resampled in pairs on the same stitches.
