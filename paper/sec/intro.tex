\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Detailed expert assessment of surgical skills can identify specific technical
errors, but it is time-consuming and ratings may vary across reviewers. The
End-to-End Assessment of Suturing Expertise (EASE)~\cite{Haque2022} provides
a structured rubric for robotic suturing, with explicit criteria for
individual sub-skills. These criteria indicate what to improve, but scoring
individual stitches manually is labor-intensive.

Automated assessment from surgical video has progressed from expertise
classification on JIGSAWS~\cite{Funke2019,Liu2021} to activity-specific skill
assessment~\cite{Kiyasseh2023} and EASE sub-skill
scoring~\cite{Cui2024,Deo2026}. These models, as well as general-purpose
vision--language models (VLMs), can predict ratings directly without
explicitly measuring the quantities underlying the assessment criteria.
Interpretability methods highlight influential kinematic
segments~\cite{IsmailFawaz2019} or relevant video
frames~\cite{Kiyasseh2023b}, while geometric methods track instrument
keypoints~\cite{Ghanekar2025} or estimate 3D needle
poses~\cite{Chiu2022,Li2024}. However, these approaches do not translate
needle--instrument geometry into quantitative measures linked to explicit
assessment criteria.

EASE defines several needle-hold criteria geometrically: the grasp position
along the needle, the angle between the needle and the instrument jaws, and
the grasp depth within the jaws. We therefore measure rather than predict:
the proposed method measures these quantities from monocular suturing videos
within the relevant evaluation intervals and maps them to EASE scores
(Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}).

Our contributions are threefold. (1) A measurement-based formulation of EASE
scoring that maps geometric measurements from event-defined windows to EASE
scores, with each score traceable to its frames, keypoints, and measured
values. The perception module is replaceable and requires no task-specific
training. (2) Calibration-free monocular estimation of the three needle-hold
quantities using 3D needle-arc fitting and weak-perspective geometry. (3)
Surgeon-disjoint validation on JIGSAWS~\cite{Gao2014}, showing that fully
automated raw-video scoring matches or exceeds direct VLM grading, even with
annotated windows and scored examples given to the VLM.
