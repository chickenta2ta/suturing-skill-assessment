\begin{abstract}
Surgical training needs objective assessment of suturing skill, and rating
tools such as EASE score how a surgeon holds the needle against defined
criteria. Rating every stitch by hand is costly, and existing automation
predicts the scores end to end, so a score cannot be checked. We instead
measure what the criteria define. Detected events divide a monocular suturing
video into stitches and into the window that each criterion refers to. In
that window, keypoints and needle masks, obtained from a general-purpose
vision--language model with a few examples, give the geometry of the needle
and the instrument, from which the grasp position, the hold angle, and the
hold depth are measured in 3D and scored by the EASE criteria. Every score is
thus traceable to the frames and the value behind it. On JIGSAWS, running
fully automatically from the raw video to the scores, the method reproduces a
surgeon's EASE scores with AUC 0.89--0.97, at least as well as direct grading
by a state-of-the-art VLM.
\end{abstract}
