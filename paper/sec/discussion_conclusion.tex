\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion_conclusion}

Geometric measurements from monocular suturing videos reproduced EASE
needle-hold scores, matching or exceeding a state-of-the-art VLM on all
three sub-skills even when it received annotated windows and scored examples.
The advantage was clearest for Needle Hold Angle, possibly because 3D
estimation is less viewpoint-dependent than judging 2D appearance. Automatic
event detection had little effect.

Most errors occurred near EASE score thresholds, though Depth of Needle Hold
also showed errors farther away, possibly because jaws spanning only a few
tens of pixels amplify the keypoint errors in Table~\ref{tab:perception}. Even incorrect scores can be traced to
their underlying measurements, source frames, and keypoints. The method
requires no task-specific training: a replaceable general-purpose VLM handles
perception, while geometry and scoring follow EASE criteria. This may ease
application to other suturing videos with limited annotations.

Limitations include a single physician rater: although EASE has
validated inter-rater reliability~\cite{Haque2022}, agreement on our data was
not measured. The test set covers only four held-out surgeons, and keypoint
and mask detection may degrade in vivo, although perception can be improved
independently.

In conclusion, event-guided measurements in sub-skill-specific windows map
EASE-defined quantities to traceable needle-handling scores.
