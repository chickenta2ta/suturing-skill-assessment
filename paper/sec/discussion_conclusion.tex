\section{Discussion and conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion_conclusion}

The results show that EASE scores can be reproduced from a monocular suturing
video by measuring what EASE defines, rather than by asking a model for a
score. Even against a state-of-the-art VLM given the annotated windows and
scored examples, our method is at least on par for every hold sub-skill and
clearly better for Needle Hold Angle, and detecting the events as well hardly
changes the results. The gain for Needle Hold Angle follows from the design:
the angle seen in the image changes with the view, so a judgment from
appearance misses poor holds, whereas the angle lifted to 3D does not depend
on how the needle and the instrument happen to be tilted.

Fig.~\ref{fig:values} shows what lies behind each score. For every
ground-truth score, the measured values gather within the range that EASE
gives that score, and most wrong scores lie just outside it: 15 of the 17
wrong scores of Needle Hold Ratio are within 0.05 of a threshold, and 9 of the
10 of Needle Hold Angle within 10$^\circ$. Most errors are thus near misses at
the boundary between two scores. Because each score is a value on the scale of
EASE, such a case can be checked by looking at the frames and the points it was
measured from. Depth of Needle Hold has more errors away from a threshold, 8 of
17, likely because the jaws span only a few tens of pixels, so that a small
shift of a keypoint moves the depth across a threshold.

The method needs no training on annotated surgical data. The events,
keypoints, and masks come from a general-purpose VLM given a few examples, and
any detector could take its place, while the geometry and the scoring rules
follow EASE and do not depend on the data. This should ease the transfer to
other suturing videos, for which annotated data are scarce.

This study has limitations. The EASE scores were given by a single licensed
surgeon. EASE has shown moderately high inter-rater reliability among trained
raters~\cite{Haque2022}, but the agreement on our data was not measured, and
confirming the results with several raters remains future work. Because the
eight surgeons of JIGSAWS were split to prevent leakage, the test set covers
four independent surgeons, each with several trials; a larger and more varied
group of surgeons is needed to confirm that the results generalize. Finally,
the videos show suturing on a bench-top model, which is easier to observe than
surgery in vivo, where bleeding, tissue deformation, smoke, and occlusion make
the keypoints and masks harder to obtain. Since the framework leaves the
perception replaceable, such videos can be addressed by strengthening that
part alone.

We presented an event-guided method that scores the EASE needle-handling
sub-skills by measuring, within the window each sub-skill refers to, the
quantities that EASE defines. It reproduces the EASE scores of a surgeon at
least as well as direct grading by a state-of-the-art VLM, and every score can
be traced back to the frames and the value it was measured from.
