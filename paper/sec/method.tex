\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Event-guided assessment framework}
\label{ssec:framework}

The four EASE needle-handling sub-skills assess different phases of
stitching: Needle Repositions
concerns preparation for the next stitch, whereas the three needle-hold
sub-skills assess the hold from the last reposition end to tissue entry.
Whole-video or whole-stitch intervals would include irrelevant
frames. We therefore detect events that delimit each stitch and assess each
sub-skill only within its relevant interval.

After the needle exits the tissue, the surgeon typically repositions it by
grasping it with both instruments and then releasing one; the final
reposition fixes the hold until entry. We use four event types:
\emph{reposition start} (both instruments grasp the needle outside the
tissue), \emph{reposition end} (one of them releases it), \emph{entry} (the
needle tip enters the tissue), and \emph{withdrawal end} (the needle leaves
the tissue on the far side). A stitch ends at withdrawal end, and its entry
$e_i$ is the last preceding entry. These events define the reposition window
$R_i=(w_{i-1},e_i]$ and hold window $W_i=[a_i,e_i]$
(gray in Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}), where $w_{i-1}$ is the previous withdrawal end
(video start for the first stitch) and $a_i$ the last reposition end in $R_i$
(or $w_{i-1}$ if none). As the hold remains fixed in $W_i$, hold sub-skills
are measured there. Following EASE, Needle Repositions scores 3, 2, or 1 for
$n_i\leq2$, $n_i=3$, or $n_i\geq4$ reposition starts in $R_i$.

\subsection{Per-frame observations}
\label{ssec:observations}

Each frame in the hold window is given a needle mask $\mathcal{M}$ and
keypoints: the needle tip $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{tip}}$ and tail
$\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{tail}}$, and, for each instrument, the jaw tip
$\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{jaw}}$ (the midpoint of the two jaw tips), the jaw base
$\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{base}}$, and, when the jaws grip the needle, the grasp
point $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{grasp}}$. The hold sub-skills use the frames in
which only one instrument grips the needle. The framework does not prescribe
how these observations are obtained; here, they and the events all come from
a general-purpose VLM given a few examples, without any training
(Sec.~\ref{ssec:implementation}).

\subsection{Needle Hold Ratio}
\label{ssec:ratio}

EASE accepts holds between the 1:1 and 3/4:1/4 positions along the needle,
but distinguishes scores 3 and 2 by adjustment to the bite, which grasp
position alone cannot reveal. Following AutoEASE~\cite{Deo2026}, we therefore
classify Needle Hold Ratio as good (EASE 2--3) or poor (EASE 1). Let
$s_i\in[0,1]$ be the tip-to-grasp arc length normalized by needle length (0
at the tip, 1 at the tail); a hold is good when $1/2\leq s_i\leq3/4$.

Because the needle is curved, partly occluded, and foreshortened, its arc
length cannot be measured directly from the image. We model it as a 3D
circular arc of radius $r$ and arc angle $\theta$, with grasp position $s_i$.
In each frame $t$, under weak perspective, we align the projected tip and
grasp point with observations, then rotate the arc about them to best fit the
needle mask and visible tail. The residual pixel discrepancy is
$E_t(r,\theta,s_i)$. Since the same needle is used throughout a video, $r$
and $\theta$ are shared across stitches and estimated jointly with their grasp
positions,
\begin{equation*}
\hat{r},\hat{\theta},\{\hat{s}_i\}=\arg\min_{r,\theta,\{s_i\}}
\sum_i\frac{1}{|F_i|}\sum_{t\in F_i}E_t(r,\theta,s_i),
\end{equation*}
where $F_i$ is the set of frames of $W_i$ in which the needle tip and mask are
observed. No needle size, needle shape, or camera calibration is given; the
stitches with a clearly visible needle constrain its shape for the others.

\subsection{Needle Hold Angle}
\label{ssec:angle}

EASE illustrates the hold angle between the grasp-to-tip needle direction
and the jaws, scoring 3 at 90--135$^\circ$, 2 at 135--150$^\circ$, and 1
otherwise. Its apparent 2D angle can change with needle or instrument tilt
(e.g., when turning the needle toward tissue before entry), even if the hold
is fixed.

We estimate the 3D angle using segment foreshortening under weak perspective,
as in uncalibrated single-image 3D reconstruction~\cite{Taylor2000}. The
needle segment
$\mathbf{u}_t=\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{tip}}-\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{grasp}}$ retains
its 3D length within each hold window, and the jaw segment
$\mathbf{v}_t=\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{base}}-\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{jaw}}$
throughout the video. Assuming approximately constant image scale, we
estimate their 3D lengths up to scale, $L_u$ and $L_v$, from high percentiles
of projected lengths within the hold window and across all hold windows,
respectively. Foreshortening gives the magnitude of each depth component,
while its sign is assigned assuming the tissue-facing needle tip is farther
from the camera than the grasp point and the jaw base is nearer than the jaw
tip. Each segment is lifted to 3D as
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_t&=\Bigl(\mathbf{u}_t,\,
\sqrt{\max(0,L_u^2-\|\mathbf{u}_t\|^2)}\Bigr),\\
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_t&=\Bigl(\mathbf{v}_t,\,
-\sqrt{\max(0,L_v^2-\|\mathbf{v}_t\|^2)}\Bigr),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
and the hold angle $\alpha_i$ is the median over the frames of the angle
between $\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_t$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_t$.

\subsection{Depth of Needle Hold}
\label{ssec:depth}

Following EASE, we score Depth of Needle Hold using the
grasp-point position $d_i$ along the jaws (0 at the tip, 1 at the base): 3 for
$d_i\leq1/4$, 2 for $1/4<d_i\leq1/2$, and 1 otherwise.

Under weak perspective, length ratios along a line are preserved between 3D
and the image, so depth requires no 3D reconstruction. When the jaws point
toward the camera, however, they appear only a few pixels long, amplifying
keypoint errors. Since the hold remains fixed until entry, we measure depth in
the frame with the longest apparent jaws, rather than
training a detector on annotated ideal frames as in~\cite{Deo2026}.
