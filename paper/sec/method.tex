\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

As outlined in Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}, the method divides a suturing video
into stitches by detecting events, and each stitch into the window that each
sub-skill refers to. Within these windows, each frame is given keypoints and a
needle mask, from which the quantities that EASE refers to are measured
geometrically and converted into scores. Every score thus traces back to a time
interval, the frames used, and a measured value.

\subsection{Event-guided assessment framework}
\label{ssec:framework}

Our goal is to assess EASE needle-handling sub-skills from a suturing video
alone. Each sub-skill, however, refers to a different part of a stitch. Needle
Repositions concerns how the needle is prepared for the next stitch, whereas
Needle Hold Ratio, Needle Hold Angle, and Depth of Needle Hold concern how the
needle is held when it is driven into the tissue. Scoring a whole video, or a
whole stitch, therefore mixes in frames that are irrelevant to the sub-skill.
We instead detect events that delimit each stitch, and assess each sub-skill
only within the interval it refers to.

A stitch generally proceeds as follows. After the previous needle has left the
tissue, the surgeon passes the needle between the two instruments to prepare
for the next stitch. The last of these repositions fixes the hold, which is
kept until the needle enters the tissue. We detect the five types of events
in Table~\ref{tab:events}, each an instant in the video. A stitch ends at a
withdrawal end, and its entry $e_i$ is the last entry before it; a retraction
does not end the stitch.

\begin{table}[htb]
\centering
\small
\caption{Event types.}
\label{tab:events}
\begin{tabular}{@{}ll@{}}
\hline
Event & Definition \\
\hline
Reposition start & Both instruments grasp the needle outside tissue \\
Reposition end & One instrument releases the needle \\
Entry & The needle tip enters the tissue \\
Withdrawal end & The needle leaves the tissue on the far side \\
Retraction end & The needle is pulled back out on the entry side \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Two observation windows follow from these events (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). The reposition
window $R_i=(w_{i-1},e_i]$ spans from the previous withdrawal end $w_{i-1}$
(the start of the video for the first stitch) to the entry. The hold window
$W_i=[a_i,e_i]$ starts at the last reposition end $a_i$ in $R_i$, or at
$w_{i-1}$ if there is none. Since the hold does not change until entry, the
hold sub-skills can be measured from any frame in $W_i$. Following EASE,
Needle Repositions is scored from the number $n_i$ of reposition starts in
$R_i$: 3, 2, and 1 for $n_i\leq2$, $n_i=3$, and $n_i\geq4$, respectively.

\subsection{Per-frame observations}
\label{ssec:observations}

To measure the hold geometrically, each frame in the hold window is given
keypoints and a needle mask $\mathcal{M}$. The keypoints are the needle tip
$\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{tip}}$ and tail $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{tail}}$, and, for
each instrument, the jaw tip $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{jaw}}$ (the midpoint of the
two jaw tips), the jaw base $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{base}}$, and, when the jaws
grip the needle, the grasp point $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{grasp}}$. The hold
sub-skills use the frames in which only one instrument grips the needle. The
framework does not prescribe how these observations are obtained; a detector
and a segmentation model trained on local annotations would serve equally
well. In this work, both the events and the per-frame observations are
obtained from a general-purpose VLM given a few examples, without any training
(Sec.~\ref{ssec:implementation}). The framework can thus be set up without
task-specific training, which should ease its transfer to other suturing
videos.

\subsection{Needle Hold Ratio}
\label{ssec:ratio}

EASE accepts a hold between the 1:1 and the 3/4:1/4 positions along the
needle, and gives 3 rather than 2 when the grasp position is adjusted to the
bite, which the position alone does not show. We therefore score it in two
classes, good (EASE 2--3) and poor (EASE 1), as AutoEASE~\cite{Deo2026} scores
every sub-skill in two classes. We measure the grasp position $s_i\in[0,1]$,
the length along the needle from its tip to the grasp point divided by the
length of the needle (0 at the tip, 1 at the tail). The hold is good when
$1/2\leq s_i\leq3/4$.

The grasp position cannot be read from the image directly. The needle is
curved, partly hidden by the jaws, and foreshortened as it tilts, so lengths in
the image are not lengths along the needle. We instead estimate the needle in
3D. The needle is modeled as a circular arc with radius $r$ and arc angle
$\theta$, with the grasp point at $s_i$ along it. In each frame $t$, the arc is
placed in 3D under weak perspective so that its tip and grasp point project
onto the observed ones, and is rotated about them so that its projection best
explains the rest of the observed needle, namely the mask and the tail when it
is visible. Let $E_t(r,\theta,s_i)$ be the remaining discrepancy in pixels.
Since one needle is used throughout a video, $r$ and $\theta$ are shared by
all its stitches and are estimated together with the grasp positions,
\begin{equation}
\hat{r},\hat{\theta},\{\hat{s}_i\}=\arg\min_{r,\theta,\{s_i\}}
\sum_i\frac{1}{|F_i|}\sum_{t\in F_i}E_t(r,\theta,s_i),
\label{eq:arc}
\end{equation}
where $F_i$ is the set of frames of $W_i$ in which the needle tip and mask are
observed. No needle size, needle shape, or camera calibration is given
beforehand; everything is estimated from the monocular video alone, and the
stitches in which the needle is clearly visible constrain its shape for the
others.


\subsection{Needle Hold Angle}
\label{ssec:angle}

EASE illustrates the hold angle as the angle between the needle, from the
grasp point toward the tip, and the jaws. It gives 3 for 90--135$^\circ$, 2
for up to 150$^\circ$, and 1 otherwise. This is an angle in 3D. The angle
seen in the image changes whenever the instrument or the needle tilts, for
example as the needle is turned toward the tissue before entry, even though
the hold itself does not change. A 2D angle therefore measures the view as
much as the hold.

We recover the 3D angle from the monocular video, based on one simple fact:
projection can only shorten a segment. The needle segment
$\mathbf{u}_t=\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{tip}}-\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{grasp}}$ keeps its
3D length throughout the hold window, and the jaw segment
$\mathbf{v}_t=\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{base}}-\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{jaw}}$ keeps its
length throughout the video. Their true lengths $L_u$ and $L_v$ are thus
revealed by the frames in which they appear longest, and we take a high
percentile of their image lengths, over the hold window for the needle and
over all hold windows of the video for the jaws. Under weak perspective, how
much shorter a segment appears gives the size of its depth component, and the
viewing geometry gives its sign: the camera looks down on the pad, so the
needle tip, pointing toward the tissue, is farther than the grasp point, and
the jaw base is nearer than the jaw tip. Each segment is lifted to 3D as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_t&=\Bigl(\mathbf{u}_t,\,
\sqrt{\max(0,L_u^2-\|\mathbf{u}_t\|^2)}\Bigr),\\
\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_t&=\Bigl(\mathbf{v}_t,\,
-\sqrt{\max(0,L_v^2-\|\mathbf{v}_t\|^2)}\Bigr),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:lift}
\end{equation}
and the hold angle $\alpha_i$ is the median over the frames of the angle
between $\tilde{\mathbf{u}}_t$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{v}}_t$. No depth sensor,
depth estimation network, or camera calibration is involved; the 3D angle
follows from how much the needle and the jaws shrink in the image.
