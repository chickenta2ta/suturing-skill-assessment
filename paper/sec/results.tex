\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Table~\ref{tab:main} compares our method with direct VLM grading using
annotated assessment windows. Our method achieved higher AUC and balanced
accuracy for all three hold sub-skills. For Needle Hold Angle, the AUC
difference was $+0.119$ (95\% CI 0.047--0.226), with EASE 1 recall of 0.83
versus 0.58 for the VLM. For the other two sub-skills, the AUC-difference
confidence intervals included zero. Applying our EASE thresholds to the
VLM's own quantity estimates reproduced its direct scores on every stitch
(Table~\ref{tab:main}), so the difference lies in the estimated quantities,
not in the scoring rule. Our method uses frames at 10 fps and the baseline at
5 fps; to show that this difference does not drive the comparison, we
restricted our method to the baseline's 5-fps frames, which changed its test
AUCs by less than 0.04 and kept the differences (Ratio $+$0.094 [0.027,
0.180], Angle $+$0.111 [0.045, 0.204], Depth $+$0.034 [$-$0.035, 0.114]).

Table~\ref{tab:e2e} reports end-to-end scoring with automatic event detection
(F1 = 0.89). All annotated stitches were matched to detections (median entry
error 0.28~s), with one extra detection. AUCs differed by at most 0.012 from
annotated-window scoring, and all four sub-skills were scored
automatically from video alone.

For each ground-truth score, measurements clustered within its EASE-defined
range (Fig.~\ref{fig:values}). Of the misclassified stitches, 15 of 17 for
Needle Hold Ratio and 9 of 17 for Depth of Needle Hold were within 0.05 of a
threshold; 9 of 10 for Needle Hold Angle were within 10$^\circ$.

The physician who rated the stitches also annotated keypoints and needle masks
in 46 frames of one test trial (Table~\ref{tab:perception}). Needle tip and
jaw tip lay within 2 px (median), whereas the jaw base, the least precise
point, was shifted toward the wrist by 0.21 jaw lengths (median). The needle
mask reached a mean surface Dice~\cite{Nikolov2021} of 0.81 at a 3-px
tolerance, half the needle width, over the 34 frames with a visible needle, a
boundary metric suited to thin structures~\cite{MaierHein2024}.

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} compares simpler measurements from the same
keypoints. In 2D, the result depended on the frame: measuring over all frames
lowered the AUC by 0.06--0.19, selecting the least foreshortened frame
recovered most of it, and the 2D angle's AUC fell from 0.91 in the first frame
of a hold to 0.41 in the last as the needle turned toward the tissue. The 3D
estimates were more robust to the viewpoint: they used all frames, varied less
within a hold, and kept an AUC of 0.91--0.92 from the first frame to the last.
